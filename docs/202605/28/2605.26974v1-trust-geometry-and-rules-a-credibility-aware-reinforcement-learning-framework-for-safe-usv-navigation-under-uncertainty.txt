# Trust, Geometry, and Rules: A Credibility-Aware Reinforcement Learning Framework for Safe USV Navigation under Uncertainty 

Yuhang Zhang, Shuqi Chai, Yukang Zhang, Liusha Yang, Member, IEEE , Mingchuan Zhang, Wei Wang, Qingjiang Shi and Quanbo Ge 

Abstract —Autonomous navigation of Unmanned Surface Ve-hicles (USVs) that is safe and compliant with the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea (COLREGs) remains a formidable challenge in dynamic maritime environments, partic-ularly when perception systems exhibit miscalibrated uncertainty. Existing Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based methods often falter because state-estimation errors induce unreliable belief states that mislead the value function, while discrete traffic rules introduce discontinuity in the learning objective. To address these challenges, we propose a framework integrating credibility-aware learning, geometric safety shielding, and continuous rule-aware embedding. First, Credibility-Weighted Value Learning (CW-VL) introduces a dynamic trust factor derived from the discrepancy between filter-estimated covariance and empirical error statistics to modulate the critic’s heteroscedastic loss, preventing policy overfitting to noisy samples. Second, the Covariance-Inflated Velocity Obstacle (CI-VO) maps position-estimation uncertainty into set-wise angular margins, forming a conservative geometric shield that overrides hazardous exploratory actions. Third, Risk-Aware COLREGs Duty Embedding relaxes binary encounter duties into continuous rule-aware signals, providing smooth sector-transition information and suppressing oscillation from sparse rule rewards. Simulated encounter studies demonstrate improved training robustness against perceptual inconsistency and superior collision avoidance and COLREGs compliance over baselines. 

Index Terms —Unmanned Surface Vehicles, Deep Reinforcement Learning, Perception Uncertainty, Velocity Obstacle, Credibility-Aware Learning

How-ever, their safe deployment in congested, mixed-traffic scenarios remains challenging. Since onboard perception systems are vulnerable to sensor noise, occlusions, and environmental disturbances, perceiving other vessels involves high uncertainty. This unreliability makes robust collision avoidance strategies imperative for ensuring safe USV operations. Collision avoidance for USVs is generally categorized as local path planning [3]. The Artificial Potential Field (APF), a classical reactive method initially introduced for robot navigation [4], is widely adopted for USVs due to its mathe-matical simplicity [5]. However, APF suffers from the inherent problem of local minima. To incorporate vehicle kinematics, the Dynamic Window Approach (DWA) is frequently employed [6] as it ensures generated commands are feasible for the ship dynamics [7]. Search-based methods, such as Hybrid A∗,are also utilized for unstructured environments where non-holonomic constraints are critical [8], but their search efficiency decreases significantly in dynamic settings. In contrast, the Ve-locity Obstacle (VO) method offers a computationally efficient geometric solution that instantly predicts collision risks [9], [10]. Nevertheless, VO methods can result in oscillatory behaviors and do not explicitly optimize energy or smoothness. Model Predictive Control (MPC) formulates collision avoidance as a constrained optimization problem to handle dynamics and constraints simultaneously [11], but the high computational complexity of solving nonlinear optimization problems online remains a bottleneck. To overcome these limitations, recent re-search has shifted toward synergizing artificial intelligence with traditional methods [12]. For instance, Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has been combined with Reciprocal Velocity Obstacles (RVO) [13], [14] or improved A∗ algorithms [15] to enhance path smoothness and search efficiency. Other approaches utilize Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) and Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) to map system states directly to control commands [16], [17], or employ feature-enhanced dynamic training to handle under-actuated USVs [18]. Furthermore, hybrid frameworks integrating Q-learning with Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) have been explored to dynamically refine evasion strategies [19]. Despite these advances, a critical limitation persists in these methodologies. Most existing approaches overlook the perception uncertainty regarding target ships and potential calibration errors in the perception system. While some methods employ heteroscedastic regression to model data noise [20], they tend to overestimate aleatoric uncertainty by failing to

distinguish between inherent sensor noise and the mismatch between the filter’s theoretical model and the actual physical system [21]–[23]. Furthermore, conventional particle-based or Gaussian-based probabilistic inference methods typically provide only probability distributions rather than deterministic bounded belief regions, which limits their efficacy in safety-critical decision-making [24]. These errors induce unreliable belief states and consequently mislead the critic network [25], causing it to overfit to noisy data or yield overly conservative estimates that degrade system reliability. In parallel, strict adherence to the convention on the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea (COLREGs) is mandatory [26], [27]. While recent works have explicitly constrained maneu-vering under COLREGs duties via risk-informed safe RL [28], [29], most approaches still encode rules through discrete sector classification or binary duty indicators [30]. This discrete representation introduces abrupt switching logic at sector boundaries. Such discontinuities create non-smooth training signals for gradient-based RL and often lead to oscillatory behaviors or unstable convergence. Motivated by the need to bridge the gap between noisy perception, rigid rules, and robust decision-making, this paper proposes a unified Credibility-Aware Safe Reinforcement Learning framework. We formulate the navigation task as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) and introduce a holistic architecture that integrates belief reliability, geometric shielding, and continuous rule embedding. The specific contributions of this work are as follows:  

> •

To mitigate the impact of unreliable perception, we propose Credibility-Weighted Value Learning (CW-VL). Unlike standard heteroscedastic approaches that often overestimate aleatoric uncertainty, we introduce a dynamic trust factor grounded in the concept of filter credibility. This factor quantifies the degree of matching between the filter’s theoretical error model and the actual physical system dynamics. By modulating the critic loss with this trust factor, we effectively down-weight samples where the filter’s internal assessment diverges from reality, prevent-ing the value function from overfitting to miscalibrated uncertainty data.  

> •

We develop the Covariance-Inflated Velocity Obstacle (CI-VO) to ensure safety under spatial uncertainty. This module explicitly maps the belief state covariance ellipse into a set-wise angular margin, creating a conservative, uncertainty-aware forbidden velocity set that serves as a geometric shield to correct hazardous exploratory actions.  

> •

We introduce a Risk-Aware Continuous COLREGs (RC-COLREGs) Embedding to facilitate stable policy learning. By relaxing binary collision regulations into continuous rule-aware signals using sigmoid-windowed sectors and a soft temporal risk gate, this mechanism provides smoother training signals near sector boundaries, avoiding the oscillation typically caused by sparse rule rewards. The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section II formulates the USV navigation task as a POMDP. Section III presents belief-state estimation and credibility-weighted PPO. Section IV introduces CI-VO, RC-COLREGs embedding, and reward design. Section V provides experimental validation and comparative studies. Section VI gives the concluding remarks and future work. II. As formulated in Section II, the USV collision avoidance task under sensor noise constitutes a multi-objective optimization problem modeled as a POMDP. This section focuses on the learning layer of the proposed framework. A recursive Bayesian estimator first constructs a Gaussian belief state from noisy observations, while a filter-consistency-based trust factor evaluates the credibility of this belief. The trust factor is then incorporated into Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) [33] through a credibility-weighted heteroscedastic critic, reducing the influence of unreliable belief samples on value learning. B. Credibility-Weighted PPO 

The value-learning component of the proposed framework is referred to as Credibility-Weighted Value Learning (CW-VL). It is implemented within PPO by replacing the standard value loss with a credibility-weighted heteroscedastic critic loss. PPO algorithms typically employ a homoscedastic mean square error (MSE) loss for value estimation [33], implicitly assuming that all state inputs are equally reliable. However, in our POMDP setting, the belief state is represented by Kalman-filter outputs whose credibility varies with noise-statistics mismatch and unmodeled dynamics. Regressing the critic on targets generated JOURNAL OF L ATEX CLASS FILES, VOL. 14, NO. 8, AUGUST 2021 5

B. RC-COLREGs Duty Embedding 

Strict COLREGs adherence is mandatory for autonomous navigation. COLREGs rules are discrete, defining binary obligations based on relative geometry. Directly incorporating such discrete logic into gradient-based reinforcement learning introduces discontinuities in the reward landscape, causing policy oscillation and convergence instability. To address this, we propose a risk-aware continuous duty embedding that encodes the right-of-way and give-way obligations into a continuous rule-aware signal Φi,k ∈ [0 , 1] , which is augmented to the state observation space, providing a smoothly varying rule-aware prior for the policy network. To validate the performance of our algorithm, a series of experiments are conducted. First, the simulation environment and experimental setup are defined. Specifically, reproducible scenarios ranging from 1 to 6 ships with increasing difficulty are constructed, in which a stage of state estimation filtering mismatch is designed to simulate realistic sensor errors. Under these settings, the proposed filtering credibility weighted critic network is compared with RL baselines to demonstrate its advantages. Furthermore, the effectiveness of the CI-VO shield is verified regarding safety and rationality. Finally, compliance with COLREGs and the feasibility of the proposed method in complex maritime environments are analyzed.

This behavior demonstrates compliant give-way execution by turning starboard to pass astern of the target. 

D. CI-VO Shield in Complex Scenarios 

To validate the proposed method’s capability in resolving safe navigation within complex, multi-target ship environments, we conducted simulation experiments focusing on dense traffic scenarios. Unlike traditional random initialization, which often yields sparse effective interactions, the training and testing environments were constructed using a novel inverse generation mechanism based on Expected Encounter Points. This mechanism first samples potential collision points along the USV’s route and then back-calculates the initial kinematic states of target ships. This ensures that every generated scene—whether involving crossing, head-on, or coordinated encirclement topologies—poses a substantive and physically valid threat to the USV. We established a representative high-density testing scenario containing six target ships to verify the Shield’s performance in real-time constraint enforcement.
