# Lagrange: An Open-Vocabulary, Energy-Based Sparse Framework for Generalized End-to-End Driving 

Shihao Ji, HongXi Li, Zihui Song, Mingyu Li 

## Abstract 

Scaling end-to-end autonomous driving to complex, open-world environments requires perceptual models that generalize to anomalous scenarios and planners that produce kinematically valid trajectories. Existing paradigms face a distinct dichotomy between representational efficiency and generalization capacity. Dense models (e.g., occupancy networks), while geometrically robust, incur critical com-putational bottlenecks and struggle with high-level semantic reasoning. Conversely, sparse, query-based planners are efficient but reliant on closed-set definitions, rendering them vulnerable to out-of-distribution (OOD) events. Although recent Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models offer open-vocabulary reasoning, their au-toregressive, discrete token generation fundamentally conflicts with the continuous, high-frequency control requirements of vehicle dynamics. To address this, we propose Lagrange, an open-vocabulary, computationally sparse driving framework based on Masked Latent Fields (MLF). Rather than relying on dense volumetric reconstructions or closed-set query mechanisms, Lagrange ex-ploits Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to encode class-agnostic object proposals into continuous semantic visual tokens. We introduce an intent-driven masked cross-attention module that temporally filters irrelevant entities, decoding the attended tokens into an implicit continuous energy field defined over spatial coordinates. By framing decision-making as a Lagrangian action minimization problem spanning this energy field, we enforce strict compliance with vehicle kinematics while execut-ing collision avoidance. Extensive offline evaluations on both standard (nuScenes) and long-tail (CODA) benchmarks demonstrate that Lagrange establishes a promis-ing framework for robust, interpretable, and kinematically feasible open-world autonomy. 

## 1 Introduction 

The design of autonomous driving (AD) systems has increasingly shifted from modular, rule-based pipelines toward end-to-end (E2E) neural architectures, aiming to minimize the compounding errors across perception, prediction, and planning modules. As autonomous capabilities scale to unrestricted open-world environments, a fundamental dichotomy has emerged regarding the optimal representation of the driving scene: dense volumetric grids versus sparse entity formulations. Dense representations, predominantly instantiated through 3D Occupancy Networks and Bird’s-Eye View (BEV) grids, formulate the environment continuously across spatial dimensions. Although highly capable of representing arbitrary geometric boundaries, these architectures implicitly treat semantics as a black box and incur massive computational overhead. For example, dense networks fundamentally struggle to differentiate the interactive severity between structurally identical but semantically divergent volumes, such as an exhausted smoke plume versus a concrete pillar.

Conversely, the sparse paradigm circumvents such bottlenecks by representing the scene through dis-crete object queries, achieving significant inference efficiency. However, these query-based methods restrict perception to a closed-set categorical distribution, defining a predefined whitelist of class la-bels ci ∈ { Car , Pedestrian , Cyclist }. When confronted with out-of-distribution (OOD) irregularities— such as spilled loads or unrecognized exotic vehicles—sparse architectures are prone to frequent false negatives, leaving the downstream trajectory planner structurally blind to potential hazards. While recent adaptations of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models attempt to bypass these lim-itations via open-vocabulary reasoning, their architectural basis in discrete, autoregressive token generation forms a fundamental mismatch with the continuous, high-frequency physical constraints of vehicular dynamics. Direct language-to-action translation frequently leads to non-smooth execution or critical mean-seeking behaviors in multimodal state spaces. To resolve the tension between open-world semantics and continuous dynamical control, we introduce Lagrange. Taking conceptual root in classical Lagrangian mechanics, our framework integrates the vast open-vocabulary generalization of large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with the physical interpretability of Energy-Based Models (EBMs). Rather than regressing a determinate trajectory matrix or sampling discrete LLM tokens, Lagrange dynamically projects continuous context into a scalar potential energy field E(x, y ), where x and y denote the planar spatial coordinates of the environment. In this learned field, anomalous and semantically hazardous entities innately map to regions of high potential, whereas navigable spaces manifest as energy valleys. The driving task is subsequently framed as solving a Principle of Least Action optimization problem. Specifically, the primary contributions of this paper are structured around three novel components. First, we design an open-vocabulary sparse tokenizer that extracts generic, class-agnostic region proposals aligned within VLM-derived continuous latent spaces. This establishes broad out-of-distribution robustness while preserving the computational edge of object-centric sparsity. Building upon this tokenizer, we develop an intent-driven reasoner based on Masked Latent Fields (MLF). By injecting a temporal kinematic state query into a masked cross-attention mechanism, the network effectively isolates context-critical semantic entities, mimicking the selective attention prioritization characteristic of human drivers. Finally, we formulate driving trajectory generation as Lagrangian Action Minimization. By mapping the attended linguistic-visual tokens into a physical continuous energy field, we strictly constrain the vehicle’s semantic objective with bounds on kinetic energy derivatives, guaranteeing kinematically feasible and provably safe trajectories. 

## 2 Related Work 

2.1 Dense versus Sparse End-to-End Planners 

Early end-to-end formulations typically employed direct mappings from sensory inputs to vehicular control signals, severely lacking transparency and interpretability. State-of-the-art dense architectures, notably UniAD [1], resolve these issues by establishing a unified BEV feature map encompassing perception, prediction, and planning. Despite their closed-set proficiency, processing dense voxel grids or executing global attention mechanisms introduces prohibitive computational latency. To circumvent this, sparse architectures including SparseDrive [2], PETR [8], and VAD [9] constrain the operational space exclusively to discrete object queries. Lagrange inherits the inherent efficiency of query-based sparsity, yet diverges fundamentally from the paradigm by substituting closed-set categorical boundaries with open-vocabulary, dense semantic embeddings. 

2.2 Vision-Language Models in Autonomous Driving 

The emergence of foundational Vision-Language Models (VLMs), such as CLIP [6] and BLIP [7], has catalyzed advancements in open-vocabulary detection (OVD). Contemporary frameworks increasingly explore the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) and VLMs directly into the driving control loop (e.g., DriveVLM [10], LLaMA-Drive [11]). However, assigning the LLM as the terminal decision-maker—where continuous trajectories are predicted as low-frequency discrete tokens or linguistic strings—neglects the strict non-holonomic kinematic limits governing high-speed vehicular dynamics. In contrast, our proposed architecture restricts the VLM exclusively to the perceptual encoding layer acting as a generalized feature extractor, delegating explicit control derivation to a continuous energy field governed by classical kinematics. 2Multi-view Images I

2.3 Energy-Based Trajectory Optimization 

Energy-Based Models (EBMs) offer mathematically robust frameworks for delineating complex, multi-modal probability distributions. Within robotics, EBM approaches frequently pose trajectory generation as an energy minimization task achieving collision avoidance and goal alignment. Tra-ditionally, the synthesis of such fields necessitated either oracle state estimations or heavy dense semantic charting. Building upon the theoretical premise of Masked Latent Fields (MLF), our ap-proach demonstrates that comprehensive implicit energy manifolds can be systematically derived from sparse, unstructured visual tokens. Lagrange projects high-dimensional VLM semantics into continuous EBMs for reactive, full-scale driving autonomy. 

## 3 The Lagrange Architecture 

The Lagrange framework is conceptualized as a fully differentiable, closed-loop neural architecture. As illustrated in Figure 1, the system comprises three sequential modules: the VLM-Driven Sparse Tokenizer, the Intent-Driven MLF Reasoner, and the Lagrangian Energy Field Decoder. These modules systematically map multi-view input streams to a continuous mathematical field, upon which real-time trajectory optimization is executed. 

3.1 VLM-Driven Sparse Tokenizer 

Standard object detection restricts models to predicting bounded geometries conditioned on categori-cal prior probabilities p(c|x). To achieve strict out-of-distribution generalization without sacrificing the computational efficiency of box-level sparsity, we formulate perception strictly as continuous feature compression. Given a multi-camera input sequence I = {I1, . . . , IM }, where M represents the total number of camera views, we employ a class-agnostic Region Proposal Network (RPN) optimized strictly for objectness—the probability that a volume disrupts the ground plane or exhibits coherent structure. Let bi ∈ R7 denote the 3D bounding box parameters (comprising 3D center coordinates, spatial dimensions, and yaw angle) generated via multi-view stereo projection for N candidate regions. Rather than computing discrete logits, we extract Region of Interest (RoI) features and project them mapping onto a pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM). To rigorously evaluate the proposed Lagrange framework, we formulate an offline experimental protocol emphasizing open-world robustness, foundational closed-set performance, and real-time computational latency. We deploy two distinct standard benchmarks to test core competencies. The nuScenes dataset serves as the baseline for closed-set E2E driving, where trajectory quality is assessed through standard geometric metrics including L2 displacement errors at discrete temporal horizons alongside the fundamental Collision Rate (CR). Subsequently, the CODA (Corner Cases in Autonomous Driving) [4] benchmark is utilized as an extreme testing ground deliberately curated with long-tail distributional anomalies, encompassing unrecognized irregular vehicles, unclassified road debris, and erratic pedestrian behaviors. On this dataset, the primary metric is the Out-of-Distribution Collision Rate (CR OOD ), evaluating the core efficacy of the VLM-driven tokenizer. Beyond standard evaluations, we introduce rigorous stress tests to measure systematic resilience. To evaluate Zero-Shot Transferability, we deploy the model trained exclusively on nuScenes directly onto the Waymo Open Dataset, assessing domain adaptation capability without geographic or sensor-specific fine-tuning. Furthermore, we assess Robustness to Sensory Perturbations by artificially injecting visual noise and simulating camera hardware dropouts during inference. We construct a comparative analysis against established state-of-the-art paradigms. UniAD [1] rep-resents the benchmark standard for unified, dense BEV network architectures. SparseDrive [2] is selected as a leading query-based, categorical whitelist-restricted E2E planner. Finally, OpenVLA-Car [3], a generic Vision-Language-Action foundation model fine-tuned to regress discrete au-tonomous driving actions, serves to represent direct LLM-based planners.

## 6 Limitations and Future Work 

While Lagrange conceptually bridges the dichotomy between semantic open-world generalization and continuous dynamical control, structural limitations remain. The reliance on a geometry-driven region proposal network implies that purely amorphous or non-geometric hazards (e.g., expansive black ice patches, or flooded arterial roads lacking discrete boundaries) might evade visual tokenization. Future development will investigate the integration of a highly compressed, low-resolution free-space segmentation token. Serving as a continuous energy floor, this would complement the discrete VLM object tokens, securing foundational drivable area bounds. Furthermore, exploring the direct distillation of the VLM alignment module into edge-device Image Signal Processors (ISPs) could yield critical latency reductions. 77 Conclusion 

In this manuscript, we present Lagrange, a novel formulation for end-to-end autonomous driving. We challenge the assumption that high-fidelity semantic control requires dense pixel-level reconstruction. By injecting the open-vocabulary generalization properties of Vision-Language Models into a highly optimized sparse tokenization framework, we effectively address the categorical whitelist limitation encumbering traditional object-centric planners. Furthermore, by translating discrete semantic tokens into an implicit energy field via Masked Latent Fields, and dictating trajectory optimization through Lagrangian action minimization, we securely bind high-level perception within strict physical reality. Lagrange establishes a mathematically elegant, computationally viable, and rigorously interpretable foundation for realizing robust Level 3 and Level 4 autonomy.
