# Minimum Time Dubins Airplane Paths with Asymmetric Climb Rates 

Jaeyoung Lim and Giuseppe Loianno 

Abstract — Dubins airplane paths approximate the limited maneuverability of fixed-wing vehicles with minimum curvature and climb rate constraints. However, the symmetric climb rate constraints result in sub-optimal paths and conservative vehicle performance. In this work, we propose asymmetric Dubins air-plane paths , which consider asymmetric climb rates for climbing and descending. We revisit the time optimality conditions and show that the asymmetric flight path angle constraints preserve optimality. We show that by considering asymmetric climb rates, we can take advantage of full performance of the vehicle, reducing the minimum time by 71% for connecting randomly generated states. We also demonstrate that the added climb rate results in 2.8 ××× faster to find the median solution time when integrated into a sampling-based planning task on rugged terrain, due to the added feasibility. We further demonstrate the practicality of the approach with a real-world flight. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Fixed-wing and hybrid Vertical Take-Off and Landing (VTOL) aerial vehicles play a critical role in large-scale environment monitoring [1] and search and rescue [2]. This is due to the energy efficiency of using aerodynamic forces to stay airborne, making them suitable for tasks requiring long endurance and long area coverage. However, limited control authority and high operating speeds constrain their maneu-verability, imposing kinematic constraints such as minimum turning radius and climb rate limits [3]. Minimum turning radius is a common constraint in a vari-ety of non-holonomic systems, such as cars [4], underwater vehicles [5], and fixed-wing vehicles [3]. Dubins curves [6] have been widely used by providing closed-form solutions for curvature-constrained shortest paths for planning [5], [7]. Dubins airplane paths [3], [8] extend the Dubins curves into three-dimensional space, by taking into account symmetric climb rate constraints. Dubins airplane paths have been successfully deployed in real world fixed-wing navigation scenarios in confined environments [1], [9] as they are efficient to compute. However, fixed-wing vehicles typically have asymmetric climb rate constraints, with different limits when climbing versus descending. This is especially true for aerodynamically efficient high-aspect-ratio platforms, which can typically climb at much steeper angles than they can descend. Therefore, practical implementations [9] typically use a conservative lower value of the climb rate constraints to satisfy the symmetric climb rate constraint assumed by the

Dubins [6] showed that a curvature constrained shortest path in SE (2) consists of a sequence of straight lines and arcs with maximum curvature. Dubins airplane paths [3] extend the Dubins curves to three-dimensional space by arXiv:2607.15863v1 [cs.RO] 17 Jul 2026 augmenting the state space with altitude and considering an additional climb rate constraint. The difference in altitude is classified into three cases, depending on the length of the horizontally projected Dubins curve. However, the optimality analysis is valid under the assumption that climb rates are symmetric for climbing and descending. Therefore, practical deployments conservatively adopt the lower of the two climb-rate bounds [9], [10]. Asymmetric flight path angles have been considered, but at the cost of optimality [8] or large lateral detours from employing an additional Dubins path along the longitudinal axis [11], [12]. Other works have considered alternative continuous path representations such as polynomials [13], [14], but do not account for climb-rate performance limits. While these path representations may be useful, the closed form solution of Dubins curves makes 

Dubins airplane paths efficient to compute, which is benefi-cial for its use in sampling-based planners. In this work, we focus on extending the Dubins airplane path representation to accommodate asymmetric climb rate constraints to be able to exploit the full performance of fixed-wing aircraft. III. B. Implications on Planning Performance 

We evaluate the implication for a downstream path plan-ning problem of navigating through rugged terrain. The goal is to find a kinematically feasible path from the start and the goal, while staying within the distance limits ( 50 m to 

120 m ) to the terrain. A Digital Elevation Maps (DEMs) with the extent of 12 .2 km × 7.48 km with 10 m lateral reso-lution is chosen, where it uses CH1903/LV03 coordinates, with Bessel 1841 as the vertical datum and provided by SwissAlti3D [16]. We fix the start and goal circle at cstart =(2992 , −4720 , ·)[ m], cgoal = ( −2992 , 4880 , ·)[ m] relative to the center of the terrain map. The minimum turn radius of the vehicle is set at 66 .67 m , and the maximum climbrate as 6 m s −1 and descend rate 3 m s −1, considering 15 m s −1

ground speed. The Dubins airplane paths are used in an asymptoticly optimal sampling-based planner RRT* [17]. Figure 6 shows a qualitative comparison of the Dubins airplane paths between the start and the goal through a mountainous terrain after 500 s planning time. The path length with asymmetric climb rate (cyan) is 14 .37 km , while symmetric climb rate (red) is 19 .20 km . This demonstrate a significant reduction of solution path length with the added 

Fig. 9: Fixed-wing platform used for real world flight exper-iments. feasibility of the asymmetric climb rates. Note that the path more aggressively follows the terrain with the added feasibility of asymmetric climb rates from the start, but follows a similar path during descent as the climb rate constraints for descending is identical. Figure 7 shows that benchmarking the planner runs with 20 repeated runs with 300 s planning time results. The median time to find an initial solution is 50 .53 s for the asymmetric Dubins path and 139 .90 s for the symmetric Dubins path, showing that the time to find the initial solu-tion is significantly reduced when using asymmetric Dubins paths. Moreover, it can be seen that the average solution path length found with asymmetric Dubins paths are significantly shorter. The benchmark was not run more than 300 s as it is practically not useful to plan onboard with longer solve times. VII. E XPERIMENTAL RESULTS 

In this section, we demonstrate the feasibility of deploying on a fixed-wing aerial vehicle. 

A. Setup 

We use a fixed-wing platform based on a Strix Stratosurfer with a 1.7 m wingspan and 4 kg weight (Fig. 9). The platform is equipped with a Flight Management Unit (FMU), which handles low level control, and a mission computer which computes high level paths. The FMU is a Holybro Pixhawk v6x, running PX4 Autopilot v1.16 which handles state esti-mation and path following control. The FMU is connected via a 433 MHz Sik Radio module to the ground control station, and an additional 2.4 GHz remote control link to the safety pilot. For the mission computer, NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX (Eight-core (ONX 16GB) Cortex A78AE Armv8.2(64-bit)) is used, where the mission computer is connected to the FMU via UART. The mission computer runs Ubuntu 22.04 and the software is integrated into Robot Operating System (ROS) Humble. The Dubins airplane path is computation is done with Open Motion Planning Library (OMPL) [18]. To generate the reference paths, two loiter paths with turn radius of 80 m was placed with 150 m altitude difference and 

75 m lateral spacing. The Dubins airplane path was generated from sampled states along each of the loiter paths, where the minimum turn radius is 66 .67 m and the maximum climb rate 4.6 m s −1 and the descend rate 2.26 m s −1 assuming 

15 m s −1 cruise speed. The path reference r = [ p, v, κ ],where p is the closest point, t is the tangent of the path, κ is the curvature of the path, is sent to the guidance controller of the FMU to follow the reference. The flight was performed at a flat field on a moderately windy day, averaging 6.4 m s −1.

In this work, we propose asymmetric Dubins airplane paths , which consider asymmetric climbrate constraints for the Dubins airplane paths. By exploiting the asymmetry of the climb rates, we show that we can significantly reduce the path duration by exploiting the full performance of fixed-wing vehicles. Moreover, it was shown that the added feasibility results in significantly improved solve time and shorter paths for a terrain planning task. The practicality of the approach is further demonstrated by a real world flight demonstration validating the asymmetric climb rates allow extracting full performance of the vehicle. Future works will consider asymmetric Dubins airplane paths into real-time receding-horizon planning as well as consider more complex environmental effects such as wind.
