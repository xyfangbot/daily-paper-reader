# Adaptive Simulation Platform in NVIDIA Isaac Sim for Self-Aware Heterogeneous Robots

KEYWORDS 

NVIDIA Isaac Sim, Self-Aware Robots, Humanoid Robot, Arm Robot, Aerial Robot, Computational Energy, Me-chanical Energy. 

ABSTRACT 

This paper presents an adaptive simulation framework, implemented in NVIDIA Isaac Sim, for self-aware hetero-geneous robotic systems. The proposed platform enables each robot to maintain an internal predictive model of its own embodiment and its interaction with the surrounding environment, while explicitly accounting for both mechan-ical actuation and onboard computational processes. By treating mechanical and computational energy as internal state variables, the framework enables runtime estimation of the energetic consequences of robot actions. Experi-mental results across humanoid, manipulator, and aerial platforms demonstrate that computational energy consti-tutes a significant portion of total energy consumption and varies with execution speed and task demands. The pro-posed platform provides a foundation for analyzing and designing energy-aware and self-aware robotic systems in complex scenarios. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Self-awareness in robotic systems refers to the ability of a robot to construct and utilize internal models of itself and its environment in order to reason about its actions, ca-pabilities, and limitations. This concept has been widely studied as a fundamental requirement for adaptive and autonomous behavior, enabling robots to predict the con-sequences of their actions, monitor internal states, and adjust behavior accordingly Jr. (1982); Rochat (2003); Bongard et al. (2006). A prominent perspective models self-awareness as a pre-dictive process, where the robot continuously estimates its internal state and interaction with the environment through an internal simulation. In this framework, the robot main-tains a representation of itself embedded within the ex-ternal world, supporting planning and decision-making without direct physical execution Tani (2016); Friston (2010); Clark (2015). Such predictive self-models have been shown to enhance robustness and adaptability in robotic systems Bongard (2015); Kwiatkowski and Buss (2019); Chen et al. (2021). A key requirement for these internal simulations is computational flexibility. The sim-ulation must operate online while allowing its temporal horizon and informational granularity to be adjusted at runtime. This is necessary because the available compu-tational resources of a robot vary dynamically with task complexity, sensing demands, and motion speed. There-fore, a self-aware robotic system must regulate the fidelity of its internal models to maintain feasible and efficient operation under changing conditions Tani (2016); Wolfe and Roy (2021). Despite progress in self-modeling, most existing sim-ulation frameworks focus on high-fidelity external mod-eling and are primarily used offline. They often neglect the internal computational cost associated with percep-tion, planning, and control during task execution. As a result, computational energy consumption is frequently overlooked, even though it directly impacts autonomy in embedded robotic systems. In this paper, we propose a flexible simulation-based framework for self-aware robotic systems that jointly mod-els external task execution and internal computational processes. The framework explicitly accounts for both mechanical and computational energy consumption and enables runtime adaptation of simulation fidelity based on available computational resources. The platform is imple-mented in NVIDIA Isaac Sim and supports heterogeneous robot morphologies operating in a shared environment. We evaluate the proposed framework using three repre-sentative robotic platforms—a fixed-base manipulator, a mobile humanoid robot, and an aerial robot—each charac-terized by distinct dynamics and computational demands. The results demonstrate that computational energy con-sumption, which varies with execution speed and work-load, constitutes a significant portion of the total energy budget and must be considered in energy-aware analysis. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows: • Proposing a flexible simulation framework for self-aware robotic systems that operates at runtime and adapts informational and temporal granularity based on available computational resources. • Enabling dynamic adjustment of simulation fidelity through runtime feedback reflecting onboard re-source availability. 2 RELATED WORK 

Self-aware robotics is commonly framed as an agent’s ability to construct and exploit internal models of its own body, sensors, and action consequences in order to sup-port prediction, adaptation, and robust autonomous be-havior Jr. (1982); Rochat (2003); Bongard et al. (2006); Tani (2016); Friston (2010); Clark (2015). Early work in cognitive science established self-awareness as a foun-dation for intentional and adaptive behavior, while later robotics research translated these ideas into computational mechanisms that allow robots to reason about their own structure and capabilities. In robotic systems, such in-ternal models enable the anticipation of action outcomes, detection of mismatches between expected and observed behavior, and adjustment of control strategies in response to environmental or internal changes. Recent studies have demonstrated that data-driven self-modeling approaches can effectively support planning and adaptation under changing morphology, damage, or un-certain dynamics Kwiatkowski and Buss (2019); Chen et al. (2021); Kwiatkowski (2022). By learning internal representations directly from sensory experience, robots can update their self-models without explicit prior knowl-edge of their physical configuration, increasing resilience and autonomy. From this perspective, internal simulation becomes an integral component of autonomous operation rather than a purely offline design or analysis tool. Such simulations must therefore operate continuously and in real time, under the computational and energy constraints imposed by onboard hardware. This requirement moti-vates resource-aware self-modeling and control strategies that explicitly account for limited internal processing re-sources as part of the robot’s operational self-awareness and decision-making processes Wolfe and Roy (2021). In parallel, energy modeling in robotics has progressed along three complementary directions. First, mechanical energy modeling has been extensively studied for mo-bile robots and manipulators using analytical or hybrid models of robot dynamics, actuation, and frictional ef-fects Tokekar et al. (2014); Liu et al. (2018); Othman et al. (2015); Shivam and Hsiao (2023); Petrichenko et al. (2024); Soori et al. (2023). Second, computa-tional energy estimation has been developed mainly in embedded and many-core computing, including runtime power/thermal/reliability-aware management and cali-brated architecture-level power models Haghbayan et al. (2017, 2023); Zhai et al. (2021); Nasser et al. (2020). How-ever, processor-centric models alone do not capture how robotic workloads (perception, planning, control) vary with sensing modality and task/environment complexity (e.g., event-based perception pipelines) Furmonas et al. (2022); Gallego et al. (2022). More recently, joint mechanical–computational treat-ments have emphasized that embodied autonomy cou-ples motion, sensing, and computation: mechanical decisions affect sensing/processing rates, while com-pute limits can constrain feasible control bandwidth and speed Mohamed et al. (2021); Shahsavari et al. (2024). In this direction, our recent works explicitly consider co-management of mechanical and computational en-ergy, including simulation-based joint power estima-tion Naseri et al. (2025a) and reinforcement-learning-based co-management policies Naseri et al. (2025b). While these efforts establish the importance of cyber– physical energy coupling, they are typically validated on a single robot/task setup. Finally, high-fidelity simulators such as NVIDIA Isaac Sim support physically based interaction and heteroge-neous robot assets, enabling reproducible evaluation in shared environments Makoviychuk et al. (2021); Mittal et al. (2023); Fichtl et al. (2022); NVIDIA (2026). Most existing Isaac Sim studies emphasize perception, learn-ing, or sim-to-real transfer rather than self-aware internal modeling that accounts for both mechanical and computa-tional energy. In contrast, our work targets a unified Isaac Sim framework that treats onboard computation as part of the robot’s internal state and enables consistent mechan-ical+computational energy accounting across heteroge-neous robots, providing a foundation for future extensions to multi-robot and swarm scenarios. 

3 PROPOSED MODEL 

From a self-awareness perspective, the proposed frame-work acts as an internal predictive model of the robot’s interaction with its environment, continuously estimat-ing its energetic state during task execution. Mechanical and computational energy are treated as internal variables, enabling runtime estimation and regulation based on the consequences of robot actions. An overview of the plat-form is shown in Fig. 1. The framework is implemented in NVIDIA Isaac Sim and supports systematic analysis of en-ergy consumption across heterogeneous robots operating in shared environments. It provides a unified simulation pipeline for multiple robot morphologies, including mo-bile robots, manipulators, and aerial systems, executing tasks under realistic physical and sensing conditions. The simulator provides accurate dynamics and sensor data, while robot state information (e.g., joint torques, veloci-ties, and sensor outputs) is streamed to an internal energy monitoring module for power estimation. Mechanical energy consumption is obtained directly from joint torques and velocities, capturing the effects of robot dynamics and environmental interactions. Computa-Figure 1: Schematic of the proposed self-aware energy modeling framework tional energy is estimated using workload-aware models of perception, planning, and control processes running on the onboard computing unit. These workloads de-pend on sensing rate, algorithmic complexity, and task requirements, and are influenced by both robot motion and environment. The framework captures the coupling between mechanical and computational subsystems: robot motion affects sensing and processing demands, while computational limits constrain control frequency and mo-tion behavior. To ensure reliable operation, application-level quality-of-service (QoS) constraints are enforced, reflecting the ability of processing pipelines to handle sen-sory data at required rates. When QoS constraints are challenged by increased workload or limited resources, robot behavior and internal model fidelity can be adjusted accordingly. This feedback loop enables regulation of be-havior based on internal computational and energetic state, reflecting a key aspect of self-aware systems. In particular, adaptive adjustment of simulation fidelity and update rate allows the robot to modulate its reasoning effort according to available resources. Finally, mechanical and computational power estimates are aggregated over time to obtain task-level energy met-rics. These metrics enable consistent comparison across robots, tasks, and strategies, and support applications such as heterogeneous robot benchmarking and extensions to multi-robot and swarm systems. For experimental purposes, we utilized a physics-based heterogeneous robotic simulation environment imple-mented in NVIDIA Isaac Sim to evaluate the proposed energy-aware benchmarking framework. Figure 2 depicts the shared warehouse scenario in which three representa-tive robotic platforms operate concurrently: a fixed-base articulated manipulator (Franka arm), a mobile humanoid robot (A2D), and an aerial robot (Ingenuity). Each robot performs a distinct class of tasks while interacting with the same environment and physics backend. This uni-fied setup enables a consistent comparison of mechanical and computational energy consumption across different robot morphologies and task modalities. Figure 3 shows the mechanical, computational, and total energy consumption of the A2D humanoid robot as a func-tion of locomotion speed. The mechanical energy per dis-tance exhibits a U-shaped trend, with a minimum at inter-mediate speeds. At low speeds, higher energy is required for balance maintenance and quasi-static actuation, while at higher speeds inertial effects and increased joint torques dominate. In contrast, computational energy per distance decreases with increasing speed, as faster motion reduces execution time per unit distance despite speed-dependent processing workloads. As a result, computational energy remains a significant portion of the total energy budget, particularly at low and moderate speeds. These results highlight that computational energy is strongly coupled with locomotion speed and must be considered in energy-aware analysis. This trade-off illustrates how a self-aware robot can regulate the fidelity of its internal model to balance computational resources and task performance.

5 CONCLUSION 

This paper presented a flexible simulation-based frame-work for self-aware robotic systems in NVIDIA Isaac Sim. By integrating mechanical dynamics and onboard computation into a unified internal representation, the plat-form enables robots to estimate and regulate their behavior based on internal energy and resource state. Experimen-tal results across heterogeneous robots demonstrated that computational processes contribute significantly to total energy consumption and are closely coupled with mechan-ical action. These findings highlight the importance of in-corporating internal resource awareness into autonomous decision-making. The proposed framework provides a foundation for future research on resource-aware control and coordination in heterogeneous and multi-robot sys-tems.
